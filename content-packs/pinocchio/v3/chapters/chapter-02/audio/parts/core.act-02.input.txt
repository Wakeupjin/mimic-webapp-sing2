[with startled concern]
He promised himself that he would rest for only one minute.
Within seconds, his eyes closed and his head dropped forward.
While he slept, the little fire quietly burned through both wooden feet.
Morning sunlight found him snoring beside a pile of warm ashes.
A loud knock woke him, followed by Geppetto’s familiar voice.
Pinocchio jumped up joyfully and immediately crashed onto the floor.
He looked down and discovered that both his feet had disappeared.
“Open the door,” Geppetto called, unaware of the strange disaster.
“I cannot,” Pinocchio cried, “because somebody has eaten my feet!”
Geppetto suspected another foolish game and told him to stop joking.
Pinocchio tried to crawl, but his wooden body rolled backwards.
At last, Geppetto climbed through the window with a worried expression.
He found his son helpless on the floor and forgot his anger.
[tenderly]
Before asking questions, Geppetto lifted Pinocchio gently.
Pinocchio poured out the night’s events in one breathless confession.
He spoke of the Cricket, the mallet, the egg, and the rain.
He added several excuses that made himself sound innocent.
Geppetto listened until the room became quiet again.
“Hunger can teach what good advice could not,” he said gently.
From his pocket, he produced three small pears, his own breakfast.
Pinocchio demanded that Geppetto peel them and remove every core.
The hungry puppet ate the soft fruit and asked for more.
Geppetto silently placed the peels and cores on the table.
Pinocchio complained, then noticed how tired his father looked.
He remembered that Geppetto had returned from a cold prison cell.
Slowly, Pinocchio ate the peels, then the cores, leaving nothing behind.
“I never knew that simple food could taste so good,” he admitted.
Geppetto smiled, because gratitude had finally entered the little room.
[hopefully]
Pinocchio looked at his missing feet and began to cry.
He promised to study, work, listen, and care for his father.
Geppetto knew that promises were easier to make than to keep.
Even so, he chose hope and began carving two new feet.
He measured carefully while Pinocchio tried to remain perfectly still.
Soon, two neat wooden feet stood ready beside the workbench.
Geppetto fixed them firmly and warned against wild testing.
Pinocchio immediately danced around the room, but this time he stopped.
He hugged Geppetto and repeated his promise to attend school.
[softly, with resolve]
The next morning, Geppetto made him clothes from humble materials.
The jacket was coloured paper, the shoes were strips of tree bark.
His cap was shaped from a piece of bread and painted brightly.
Pinocchio admired himself as though he were dressed like a prince.
Then he remembered that every proper pupil needed a schoolbook.
Geppetto’s smile faded, for his pockets contained no money at all.
“Wait here,” he said, pulling on his old winter coat.
He went into the freezing street while Pinocchio practised writing his name.
Later, Geppetto returned carrying a new alphabet primer.
He was trembling, and his thin shirt offered no protection from winter.
“Where is your coat?” Pinocchio asked, already knowing the answer.
“It made me too warm,” Geppetto replied with an unconvincing laugh.
Pinocchio pressed the precious book against his wooden heart.
For once, he understood that love could be measured by what someone surrendered.
At sunrise, he stepped towards school wearing Geppetto’s sacrifice.
Yet somewhere along the road, another temptation was tuning its instruments.
