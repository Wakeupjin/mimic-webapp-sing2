The prison gate opened, and Pinocchio hurried towards the Fairy.
He felt sorry for all his broken promises.
This time, he wanted to tell no excuses.
The road crossed a quiet field in warm sunlight.
A huge green Serpent blocked the whole road.
Smoke came from its nose, and its eyes burned.
Pinocchio stopped, and his wooden legs shook.
“Please move your tail,” he asked the Serpent.
The creature did not answer or move.
Pinocchio tried to climb over its long body.
The tail rose, and Pinocchio fell into the mud.
The Serpent laughed at the muddy wooden boy.
It laughed until it fell down, so Pinocchio escaped.
Pinocchio cleaned his face and continued walking.
He had eaten nothing, and every smell made him hungry.
Purple grapes hung behind a low vineyard wall.
“I will take one bunch,” Pinocchio told himself.
He climbed over the wall and touched the grapes.
An iron trap closed around both wooden ankles.
Pinocchio pulled hard, but the trap stayed closed.
Each movement made the metal teeth hold tighter.
Cold rain fell across the dark vineyard.
Pinocchio remembered all the warnings he had ignored.
“I found a reason to steal,” he whispered sadly.
Only the small crickets heard his honest words.
A farmer finally arrived with a bright lantern.
The farmer used the trap to stop chicken thieves.
He saw Pinocchio and the damaged grape plants.
“A wooden thief is still a thief,” he said.
Pinocchio admitted that hunger had led him there.
The truth could not remove his wrong choice.
The farmer decided not to send him to prison.
Instead, he took Pinocchio to the chicken coop.
An empty kennel stood beside a heavy chain.
The farmer’s old watchdog, Melampo, had recently died.
“You will guard my chickens tonight,” said the farmer.
He locked a collar around Pinocchio’s wooden neck.
Pinocchio sat by the kennel and felt deep shame.
Now freedom seemed less important than being honest.
At midnight, four thin Weasels entered the farmyard.
They expected to see Melampo beside the coop.
Their leader spoke to Pinocchio like a business partner.
The Weasels often stole eight chickens each week.
They gave Melampo one chicken for staying quiet.
“Take the same deal,” she whispered to Pinocchio.
Pinocchio remembered the grapes and his heavy collar.
He did not want to make another dishonest choice.
However, he nodded and acted like he agreed.
The Weasels entered the coop to take the chickens.
Pinocchio shut the door and dropped the bar.
He barked until the farmer turned on a light.
The farmer came outside and found the trapped thieves.
He asked Pinocchio how he knew their secret.
Pinocchio could have blamed the dead watchdog.
Instead, he told the farmer about the full offer.
He admitted that he had tricked the Weasels.
The farmer listened and removed the hard collar.
“You stole grapes, but you saved my chickens.”
Pinocchio promised that he would never steal food again.
“Keep that promise when hunger returns,” said the farmer.
At sunrise, the farmer opened the farm gate.
Pinocchio received no prize, but he felt lighter.
Honesty changed his next step, not his past mistake.
At noon, Pinocchio reached the Fairy’s old home.
The bright house and garden were completely gone.
A small white stone stood in the wild grass.
The words said that the Blue Fairy had died.
They said Pinocchio’s desertion had broken her heart.
He fell down and held the cold stone.
“Please return, and I will be better,” he cried.
The empty field gave him no answer.
Pinocchio cried until his voice became very weak.
He remembered the Fairy’s care and patient help.
Every lost second chance now felt very precious.
The sky grew dark above the silent stone.
Pinocchio stayed with the pain he had caused.
Then he heard strong wings moving above him.
A large Pigeon landed beside the white stone.
The Pigeon knew Geppetto and knew Pinocchio too.
“Your father has searched everywhere,” the bird said.
Geppetto had walked through many towns and forests.
Then he built a little boat near the sea.
He wanted to search across the ocean for Pinocchio.
Pinocchio stood up with both hope and fear.
The sea was more than a thousand miles away.
“Climb on my back,” the kind Pigeon said.
They flew high above houses, roads, and fields.
They crossed cold clouds and rain all night.
In the morning, Pinocchio smelled the salty sea.
The Pigeon landed near a worried crowd.
A tiny boat rose and fell on huge waves.
Pinocchio saw Geppetto holding the boat’s oars.
“Father!” he shouted into the loud storm.
Geppetto saw Pinocchio and waved his old cap.
Then a dark wave crashed against the boat.
For a moment, Geppetto and the boat disappeared.
People begged Pinocchio to stay on the beach.
He remembered how far Geppetto had searched.
Pinocchio dropped his cap and entered the sea.
His wooden body floated, but the storm was strong.
He swam past the waves and called for Geppetto.
Night came, and the beach disappeared behind him.
Still, he swam towards his father’s lost boat.
Pinocchio did not know what waited ahead.
He only knew he would not leave Geppetto again.
