[British accent]
[slowing into concern]
Five months passed without school or useful work.
One morning, Pinocchio felt an itch above his head.
He touched two long and hairy ears.
At first, he thought Lamp-Wick was joking.
Then the mirror showed his own donkey ears.
Pinocchio hid them under a very large cap.
He locked the door and searched for answers.
The ears continued growing during the day.
Soon the cap could not cover them.
Pinocchio became too frightened to stay alone.
He called for help from a doctor.
An old Marmot came into the room.
She knew many illnesses of careless children.
[gravely]
She checked his ears, hands, and wooden feet.
Then her face became serious and sad.
“You have Donkey Fever,” she told him.
Children without duties slowly lost their childhood.
Soon Pinocchio would become a full donkey.
He asked for medicine or another chance.
The Marmot could only shake her head.
This change had started many months before.
Every ignored warning had made it stronger.
Pinocchio remembered the donkey under the wagon.
Now he knew that it had been a child.
After the Marmot left, Pinocchio ran outside.
He hurried towards Lamp-Wick’s small house.
[in frightened disbelief]
Lamp-Wick opened the door with a huge cap.
Both boys tried not to look closely.
Pinocchio asked Lamp-Wick to remove his cap.
Lamp-Wick told Pinocchio to go first.
They counted to three and uncovered together.
Two pairs of donkey ears stood straight up.
For a moment, both friends stayed silent.
Then nervous fear became wild laughter.
Their laughter changed into loud donkey brays.
Hands became hooves, and faces became longer.
Grey hair covered their changing bodies.
Long tails grew behind them at last.
Soon two frightened donkeys lay on the floor.
[somberly]
Outside, the wagon driver heard the braying.
He entered with ropes and locked the bedroom door.
Toyland had always hidden his secret business.
It turned careless children into animals he could sell.
Pinocchio tried to say his own name.
Only another sad donkey sound came out.
Lamp-Wick kicked, but the driver tied both boys.
He kept them side by side beneath his watch.
Pinocchio finally understood every warning on the midnight road.
The tired donkey under the wagon had once been a child.
He remembered the Fairy’s twelve empty chairs.
A promised holiday had become a terrible trap.
Another wagon of laughing children arrived outside.
Before dawn, the driver announced that the morning market waited.
Neither frightened friend could escape what morning would bring.
