[British accent]
[warmly]
The Fairy’s bright house was busy with party work.
Tomorrow, Pinocchio would become a real boy.
Small cakes, fruit, and sweets waited on a long table.
Twelve chairs stood ready around it for his school friends.
Pinocchio felt proud of his recent hard work at school.
He ran through town to invite everyone before evening.
However, he could not find his friend Lamp-Wick.
Lamp-Wick was thin, restless, and very lazy.
Pinocchio searched streets and playgrounds until the sky became dark.
At last, he found Lamp-Wick hiding beneath a farm porch.
“Please come to my party tomorrow,” Pinocchio said.
Lamp-Wick looked towards the empty road with a secret smile.
He was waiting for a special midnight wagon that would leave town.
[temptingly]
The wagon was going to the Land of Toys.
Lamp-Wick said that nobody studied or worked there.
There were no schools, books, teachers, or lessons.
Children played all day and every day.
“I will live there forever,” Lamp-Wick announced.
Pinocchio remembered the Fairy and her promise.
Still, a life of easy games sounded wonderful.
He said that he only came to invite Lamp-Wick.
Lamp-Wick asked him to wait for the wagon.
One minute became ten under the bright moon above the quiet fields.
Pinocchio promised to leave before the wagon.
Lamp-Wick kept describing the amazing place.
By midnight, Pinocchio was still beside him.
[with growing unease]
Soon they heard small bells in the distance.
A large wagon appeared through the darkness with yellow lanterns.
It was completely full of children squeezed tightly together.
Twelve tired donkeys with wet eyes pulled the heavy wagon.
Lamp-Wick climbed inside with great excitement.
There was no open seat for Pinocchio.
The driver offered him a donkey’s back.
Pinocchio remembered his party and stopped.
Then the donkey spoke softly through its bridle in a tired voice.
“Turn back before you become like me.”
The driver hit the animal and laughed as if nothing had happened.
Pinocchio saw fear inside the donkey’s eyes.
However, he climbed on for a short visit.
The wagon left the Fairy’s empty chairs behind.
[wildly joyful]
At sunrise the next morning, they entered the Land of Toys.
Bells, drums, and happy voices came from every house.
Some children raced through crowded squares while others flew large kites.
Theatres showed funny plays all day without stopping.
Sweet shops with cakes and sweets never closed their colourful doors.
Nobody carried a bag or went to work.
Pinocchio and Lamp-Wick tried every game.
They built towers, chased parades, and broke toys until they fell asleep.
The next day was exactly the same, without clocks or chores.
Soon the days became one long holiday.
Sometimes Pinocchio remembered the waiting Fairy.
He played louder because the memory hurt.
