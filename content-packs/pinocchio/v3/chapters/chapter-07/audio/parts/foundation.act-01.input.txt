[exhausted but hopeful]
Pinocchio swam until the storm became weaker.
At sunrise, the sea carried him towards an island.
He reached the rocks, cold, tired, and alone.
He could not see Geppetto or the small boat.
Pinocchio called, and a Dolphin came near.
“Did you see my father?” Pinocchio asked.
The Dolphin had seen a boat far away.
The Terrible Shark was also swimming near it.
That sea creature could swallow a whole ship.
Pinocchio felt great fear inside his wooden chest.
The Dolphin did not know if Geppetto escaped.
It showed Pinocchio a road across the island.
Pinocchio thanked the Dolphin and started walking.
[briskly]
The road entered a busy town full of workers.
Bakers carried bread, and shops opened their doors.
Builders worked while children carried small boxes.
Fresh bread made Pinocchio’s empty stomach growl.
He asked a charcoal seller for one coin.
The man offered coins for pushing his heavy cart.
Pinocchio refused because the black dust looked dirty.
Next, he asked a mason for some food.
The mason offered bread for carrying a basket.
Again, Pinocchio refused because the work looked hard.
Other people offered jobs, but nobody offered free food.
The town expected every person to help.
Soon, Pinocchio’s pride felt heavier than any basket.
[humbly]
At a fountain, he met a woman with water jugs.
She gave him fresh water to drink.
Then Pinocchio smelled food inside her basket.
“Carry one jug, and you can eat,” she said.
Her house stood at the top of a hill.
Pinocchio thought about the heavy water jug.
His hungry stomach gave him the honest answer.
He lifted the jug and followed her home.
His feet slipped, and his shoulders began to hurt.
However, he carried every drop to the house.
The woman gave him bread, vegetables, and something sweet.
The simple meal tasted wonderful after his work.
Pinocchio learnt that honest work could feel good.
[tenderly]
While eating, Pinocchio looked carefully at the woman.
Her eyes looked familiar, and her hair was blue.
“You look like an old friend,” he said.
The woman asked him to name that friend.
Pinocchio spoke about the little Blue Fairy.
“I am that Fairy, but I grew older,” she said.
Time had moved on during Pinocchio’s wild adventures.
He hugged her and called her his little mother.
The Fairy loved him but remembered his past choices.
The white memorial stone still made Pinocchio shake.
His sadness and his wish to change were real.
For the first time, he felt safe again.
He asked if Geppetto could still be alive.
