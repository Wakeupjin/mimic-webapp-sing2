Long ago, Master Cherry worked in a small Tuscan town.
He was an old carpenter with a round, red nose.
One cold morning, he found a plain block of wood.
The block stood beside his busy workbench.
It looked strong enough for a table leg.
Master Cherry picked up his axe and smiled.
Before he hit the wood, a voice cried, “Please stop!”
The carpenter froze with the axe above his head.
He looked under the bench and behind the door.
He opened every cupboard, but nobody was there.
“I heard nothing,” he said, although his legs shook.
He slowly raised the axe for a second try.
The axe hit, and the voice shouted, “That hurt me!”
Master Cherry dropped the axe and stared at the block.
Wood could not talk, but this block clearly could.
He tried to make its surface smooth with a plane.
The tool touched the wood, and the block began laughing.
Master Cherry fell backwards into a basket of shavings.
His face turned white, red, and then white again.
“Someone is playing a trick,” he whispered.
He touched the block with one careful finger.
The block stayed quiet.
Then he pressed it a little harder.
A long, unhappy sound came from inside the wood.
Just then, someone knocked loudly on the shop door.
Master Cherry nearly jumped out of his apron.
He pushed the block away and called, “Come in!”
Geppetto entered in an old coat with many patches.
He had little money, but he had big ideas.
“I have a wonderful plan,” he told his friend.
“I will make a wooden boy who can dance.”
“He will fence and turn through the air too.”
The puppet could perform and earn money for food.
But Geppetto also wanted someone beside him at home.
His one small room felt lonely every night.
“I only need a good piece of wood,” he said.
A soft laugh came from the dark corner.
Master Cherry looked at the strange block again.
He decided that Geppetto should take it.
“You can use that block,” he said quickly.
Geppetto bent down and reached for the wood.
Before he touched it, a voice called, “Hello, Polendina!”
That rude name made fun of Geppetto’s yellow wig.
Geppetto turned around with an angry face.
“Did you say that?”
Master Cherry said he had not spoken.
The two old friends did not believe each other.
They shouted, waved their arms, and stamped their feet.
Soon they rolled across the floor in a silly fight.
At last, both men stopped because they were tired.
Geppetto fixed Master Cherry’s collar.
Master Cherry brushed dust from Geppetto’s coat.
“Are we friends again?” Geppetto asked.
“Yes, friends again,” Master Cherry answered.
The block gave one small, happy laugh.
Master Cherry wrapped it and gave it to Geppetto.
On the way home, Geppetto imagined a happy crowd.
He saw the puppet dancing while people clapped.
His real home was only one cold room.
A fire painted on the wall gave no heat.
Geppetto put the block on his workbench.
Then he lit a small lamp.
“What name shall I give you?” he wondered.
After a moment, he smiled.
“Your name will be Pinocchio.”
The name sounded bright and full of fun.
Geppetto made his tools sharp and ready.
“Let us make a boy and a family,” he said.
First, he carved the hair, head, and two eyes.
The new eyes looked straight at him.
“Please look kindly at your father,” Geppetto said.
The eyes turned up towards the ceiling.
Next, Geppetto made a long nose.
He cut it shorter, but it grew again.
Then he carefully shaped a mouth.
The mouth smiled and started laughing at him.
“You have no body, but you are already rude.”
The smile became even wider.
Geppetto carved the neck, body, arms, and hands.
The new hands quickly took his yellow wig.
Pinocchio waved the wig and laughed loudly.
“Give it back,” Geppetto said with a sad voice.
He finished the wooden legs and feet.
Then Geppetto placed Pinocchio on the floor.
Pinocchio kicked his nose and pulled away.
One step became two, and then he started running.
He knocked over a stool and jumped across the bed.
“Stop!” Geppetto cried.
Pinocchio opened the door and ran outside.
He raced past shops, chickens, and laughing children.
Geppetto followed in slippers and called his name.
Pinocchio did not turn around.
Being free felt exciting, and danger felt far away.
A police officer stood in the middle of the road.
He caught Pinocchio by his very long nose.
The puppet’s wooden legs kept running in the air.
Geppetto arrived tired but happy to see him.
“Thank you for stopping my boy,” he said.
“I will take him safely home now.”
Geppetto reached for ears that he had never carved.
He held Pinocchio’s neck, so the puppet fell down.
People thought Geppetto was hurting the wooden boy.
The officer listened to the crowd, not the facts.
He let Pinocchio go and took Geppetto away.
Pinocchio ran free while his father went to prison.
“I made you with love, but not enough sense.”
Pinocchio’s first hard lesson was now very close.
