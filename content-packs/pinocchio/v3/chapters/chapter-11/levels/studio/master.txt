Leaving the resigned Tunny behind, Pinocchio followed the faint light through darkness.
Greasy water shifted beneath his feet, making every step uncertain.
Around him, the Shark’s breathing rolled like distant thunder through a cavern.
At first, the golden point remained as unreachable as a star.
Pinocchio continued because patient movement offered more hope than surrender.
Gradually, the mystery became a candle burning inside a glass bottle.
It stood upon a small table among scattered boxes and barrels.
Beside the flame sat an old man whose hair had turned white.
He wore torn clothes and ate a wriggling fish with desperate hunger.
The scene was strange, yet something about the bent shoulders felt familiar.
Pinocchio moved closer, afraid that hope might be inventing what he saw.
The prisoner raised tired eyes towards the unexpected sound of footsteps.
Within the narrow candlelight, Pinocchio recognised his missing father.
Joy weakened his knees more completely than fear ever had.
“Father, I have finally found you!” he cried across the wreckage.
Geppetto rubbed his eyes, suspicious of another dream created by loneliness.
Then Pinocchio embraced the thin neck he had remembered for so long.
They laughed and wept together, each trying to speak before breathing.
For several precious moments, explanation could wait outside their embrace.
Geppetto touched the puppet’s face as though testing an impossible miracle.
Pinocchio promised that no selfish adventure would separate them again.
“Are you truly my own Pinocchio?” Geppetto asked in wonder.
“Yes, Father, and I have searched every shore for you.”
The monster shifted, making the bottle tremble above the table.
Even here, reunion created a small room that felt like home.
Eventually, Geppetto asked how his son had travelled into the Shark.
Pinocchio began with the schoolbook purchased through his father’s only coat.
He admitted exchanging that sacrifice for admission to the marionette theatre.
Next came Fire-Eater, five gold pieces, and a hopeful road home.
The Fox and Cat had filled that road with hunger, urgency, and deception.
Pinocchio described the Field of Wonders and every lie that lengthened his nose.
He remembered the robbers, the oak tree, and the Fairy’s rescue.
Her forgiveness had repeatedly opened doors that his impulses soon closed.
He confessed prison, the watchdog’s collar, and the green fisherman’s net.
He spoke of school, fighting, the sea, and his continuing search.
On Busy Bee Island, honest labour had briefly given his days direction.
Yet even that progress had been abandoned when Lamp-Wick promised Toyland.
There, endless amusement had ended in a donkey’s body and a circus rope.
“I kept choosing excitement before the people who loved me,” Pinocchio concluded.
Geppetto listened without interruption, allowing honesty to complete its difficult work.
When the confession ended, he pulled his son close and forgave him.
Then Geppetto explained the journey that had brought him into darkness.
He had built a fragile boat and sailed away searching for Pinocchio.
A violent storm drove the little craft beyond every familiar landmark.
The waves broke it apart before the Terrible Shark swallowed Geppetto.
At first, he believed the monster’s stomach would become his grave.
Later, the Shark swallowed a merchant ship loaded with useful provisions.
Biscuits, preserved food, dry matches, and candles floated from the wreck.
Those supplies had kept Geppetto alive for almost two years.
He measured time through shrinking candles and the whitening of his beard.
Now only a few scraps of food remained beside the final flame.
Once that candle failed, direction and warmth would vanish together.
“Then we must leave before it dies,” Pinocchio answered immediately.
Geppetto studied his weakened legs and admitted that he could not swim.
Pinocchio refused to let the problem become a reason for delay.
“You carried me through childhood; tonight, I will carry you.”
The promise astonished Geppetto more than any magical event in the story.
His impulsive puppet now sounded like the practical child he needed.
Pinocchio searched the wreckage and questioned Geppetto about possible exits.
Geppetto explained that age and asthma made the Shark sleep heavily.
During those deep sleeps, its enormous mouth remained partly open.
If they moved silently, the jaws might become a doorway instead of a prison.
The shortening candle warned them that planning time had nearly ended.
Geppetto lifted the bottle while Pinocchio offered a steady supporting hand.
Together they began the slippery climb through the monster’s vast throat.
The walls tightened and released with each slow, sleeping breath.
They placed their feet carefully, terrified of waking the creature around them.
At last, cool night air touched Pinocchio’s damp wooden face.
Moonlight entered through the open jaws and silvered the enormous tongue.
That tongue stretched ahead of them like a broad country road.
Beyond three rows of teeth, calm water reflected the waiting sky.
Geppetto stared at the drop and tightened his grip on Pinocchio.
“Stay close and trust my steps,” the puppet whispered.
They approached the final edge while the Shark slept beneath them.
Suddenly, its breathing gathered into one deep and dangerous pressure.
The entire body tightened around the breath it could not contain.
The Shark sneezed with the force and noise of a cannon.
Wind flung father and son backwards across the slippery tongue.
They rolled down the throat and crashed beside the wrecked ship.
The candle struck the water, hissed briefly, and disappeared into darkness.
“Now there is no hope,” Geppetto whispered, unable to hide his fear.
Pinocchio felt the same terror but would not surrender leadership to it.
“We still have each other, and I remember the path,” he replied.
He searched for Geppetto’s hand and guided him upright again.
Without light, each step required memory, patience, and complete trust.
They climbed the long throat while the monster settled back into sleep.
This time, Pinocchio waited until every breath became slow and even.
They crossed the tongue again and passed all three rows of teeth.
At the final edge, moonlit water seemed both beautiful and bottomless.
Pinocchio crouched and asked his father to climb onto his back.
Geppetto locked both arms around the puppet’s narrow wooden shoulders.
“Hold tightly, Father,” Pinocchio said, though his own courage trembled.
He sprang beyond the teeth and plunged into the cold sea.
For one dangerous second, Geppetto’s weight dragged them both down.
Then Pinocchio’s wooden body rose, and his arms found their rhythm.
Behind them, the Shark continued sleeping beneath the peaceful moon.
Ahead, darkness hid every sign of a safe shore.
Pinocchio chose a direction by the stars and adjusted his father’s weight.
Geppetto rested against him, exhausted beyond words but finally free.
Every stroke carried them farther from the body that had imprisoned them.
The open sea remained dangerous, yet they faced its danger together.
Pinocchio had entered the Shark seeking escape and discovered responsibility.
Now the son made of wood was carrying his maker towards home.
