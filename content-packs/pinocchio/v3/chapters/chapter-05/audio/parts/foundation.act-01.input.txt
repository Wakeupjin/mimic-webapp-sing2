[British accent]
[with magical concern, then wryly]
Early that morning, the Blue Fairy sent a Falcon.
The bird cut the rope around Pinocchio’s neck.
A large Dog carried him to the white house.
The Fairy put Pinocchio beside a warm fire.
His wooden body looked weak and very still.
She called three doctors to examine him.
An Owl, a Raven, and a Cricket arrived.
The Owl said Pinocchio might be alive.
The Raven said he might be dead.
The Cricket said, “He is a disobedient boy.”
Pinocchio heard this and cried under the blanket.
His tears showed that he was truly alive.
The Fairy thanked the doctors and made some medicine.
[with comic urgency]
Pinocchio saw the dark medicine and shook his head.
The Fairy offered sugar, but he still refused.
“Bad tastes can still help you,” she explained.
Pinocchio closed his mouth as tightly as possible.
Then four Rabbits entered with a small box.
They came for a patient who refused help.
Pinocchio quickly drank all the bitter medicine.
The Rabbits left, and the Fairy smiled softly.
Soon, warmth returned to his wooden arms and legs.
“It was not as bad as I feared,” he said.
“Fear often makes small things seem large,” she replied.
Pinocchio moved four coins from his mouth into his pocket.
[knowingly]
The Fairy asked why he was alone near the oak.
Pinocchio first told her about Fire Eater.
Then he reached the part about the Fox and Cat.
“I lost all my money,” he said nervously.
At once, his nose grew across the pillow.
He changed the story and blamed some strangers.
His nose reached past the bed towards the wall.
Then he said he never had any coins.
Now wooden branches trapped him under his long nose.
The Fairy laughed gently but waited before helping.
“People can notice lies without seeing this nose,” she said.
Pinocchio admitted that four coins were safe in his pocket.
He also admitted that he wanted easy money.
[warmly]
The Fairy called Woodpeckers to shorten his nose.
Their beaks tapped until his face looked normal.
Pinocchio promised to tell the truth next time.
The Fairy said Geppetto was coming to the house.
Pinocchio jumped from the bed with sudden joy.
He wanted to meet his father on the road.
The Fairy let him go by the direct path.
“A straight road still needs a good choice,” she said.
Pinocchio thanked her and entered the forest.
He practised telling Geppetto the whole honest story.
