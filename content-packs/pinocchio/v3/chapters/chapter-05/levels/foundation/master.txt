Early that morning, the Blue Fairy sent a Falcon.
The bird cut the rope around Pinocchio’s neck.
A large Dog carried him to the white house.
The Fairy put Pinocchio beside a warm fire.
His wooden body looked weak and very still.
She called three doctors to examine him.
An Owl, a Raven, and a Cricket arrived.
The Owl said Pinocchio might be alive.
The Raven said he might be dead.
The Cricket said, “He is a disobedient boy.”
Pinocchio heard this and cried under the blanket.
His tears showed that he was truly alive.
The Fairy thanked the doctors and made some medicine.
Pinocchio saw the dark medicine and shook his head.
The Fairy offered sugar, but he still refused.
“Bad tastes can still help you,” she explained.
Pinocchio closed his mouth as tightly as possible.
Then four Rabbits entered with a small box.
They came for a patient who refused help.
Pinocchio quickly drank all the bitter medicine.
The Rabbits left, and the Fairy smiled softly.
Soon, warmth returned to his wooden arms and legs.
“It was not as bad as I feared,” he said.
“Fear often makes small things seem large,” she replied.
Pinocchio moved four coins from his mouth into his pocket.
The Fairy asked why he was alone near the oak.
Pinocchio first told her about Fire Eater.
Then he reached the part about the Fox and Cat.
“I lost all my money,” he said nervously.
At once, his nose grew across the pillow.
He changed the story and blamed some strangers.
His nose reached past the bed towards the wall.
Then he said he never had any coins.
Now wooden branches trapped him under his long nose.
The Fairy laughed gently but waited before helping.
“People can notice lies without seeing this nose,” she said.
Pinocchio admitted that four coins were safe in his pocket.
He also admitted that he wanted easy money.
The Fairy called Woodpeckers to shorten his nose.
Their beaks tapped until his face looked normal.
Pinocchio promised to tell the truth next time.
The Fairy said Geppetto was coming to the house.
Pinocchio jumped from the bed with sudden joy.
He wanted to meet his father on the road.
The Fairy let him go by the direct path.
“A straight road still needs a good choice,” she said.
Pinocchio thanked her and entered the forest.
He practised telling Geppetto the whole honest story.
Near town, he saw two figures by a fountain.
The Fox stood on one leg beside the Cat.
They greeted Pinocchio like old and trusted friends.
The Fox said dangerous men had delayed them.
The Cat hid the paw that Pinocchio had bitten.
Pinocchio asked why they left him with the bill.
They praised him and blamed some urgent business.
Then the Fox spoke about helping poor Geppetto.
The Field of Wonders was very close, he said.
Pinocchio remembered the Fairy and tried to leave.
The Fox described a tree full of gold coins.
Pinocchio imagined Geppetto living in a warm home.
That picture made his new promise feel weaker.
He chose one last shortcut for a good reason.
In Simpleton Town, hurt animals begged while rich thieves rode past.
Hungry workers watched dishonest people live well.
The town looked wrong, but Pinocchio ignored the warning.
The Fox chose a dry place in the field.
Pinocchio dug a hole and buried four coins.
He covered them and poured water on the soil.
The Fox sent him to a far stream.
When Pinocchio returned, the Fox and Cat were gone.
He waited for a golden tree to grow.
One hour passed, but the ground stayed empty.
A Parrot in a nearby tree laughed loudly.
“Why are you laughing at my fortune?” Pinocchio asked.
“Money does not grow without work,” said the Parrot.
The Fox and Cat had stolen the coins.
Pinocchio dug and found only four empty holes.
The truth hurt because others had already warned him.
Pinocchio ran to court and asked for justice.
A Gorilla judge listened to his full complaint.
Pinocchio named the thieves and explained their trick.
The judge became serious and gave his decision.
“You were foolish and lost your own money.”
“So prison will give you time to think.”
Pinocchio said a victim should not receive punishment.
The guards agreed, but they followed the order.
They locked Pinocchio inside a cold stone cell.
Real criminals laughed at this strange kind of justice.
Pinocchio now had no money, tricks, or excuses.
He thought about the Fairy and his worried father.
Twice, he had chosen a sweet story over truth.
This choice did not happen because of hunger.
He had known the trick and followed it again.
Several days passed inside the dark prison.
The criminals proudly told stories about their bad choices.
A royal celebration finally freed every criminal.
The jailer kept Pinocchio because he said he was innocent.
Pinocchio quickly called himself a terrible criminal too.
The confused jailer opened the door and freed him.
Freedom came because Pinocchio told another false thing.
He walked into daylight and searched for his family.
The lost coins had taught him only part of the lesson.
The next road would test his actions, not his words.
