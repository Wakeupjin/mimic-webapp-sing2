[British accent]
[hopefully, then tempted]
Pinocchio left home with his new alphabet book.
He planned to learn and help Geppetto later.
First he would read, write, and learn numbers.
One day, he could buy Geppetto another coat.
These good plans went with him towards school.
For a while, Pinocchio walked like a serious pupil.
Then he heard drums and pipes across the town.
His feet slowed, but he told them to continue.
“School stays all day, but music can leave,” he thought.
He would listen for one minute and return.
The music led him into a busy square.
Bright flags moved above a travelling puppet theatre.
The alphabet book suddenly felt heavy under his arm.
[with uneasy temptation]
A large sign promised battles, heroes, and funny surprises.
Pinocchio pushed through the crowd to the ticket window.
A ticket cost four pennies, but he had none.
He offered his paper coat and bark shoes.
He even offered his strange cap made of bread.
The ticket seller did not want any of them.
Pinocchio looked at the book from Geppetto’s coat.
For a moment, he remembered his cold father.
Then the drums became louder, and he forgot.
Another boy bought the book from Pinocchio.
Pinocchio used the money for a theatre ticket.
He went inside before he could feel sorry.
[joyfully]
Children sat in front of a small stage.
Pinocchio found a seat as the lights became low.
The curtain opened on Harlequin and Punchinello.
The two puppets argued in a painted garden.
The children laughed until Harlequin suddenly stopped.
He looked into the crowd and saw Pinocchio.
“Look, friends!” he cried. “A brother is here!”
Every puppet on the stage turned around.
They left the play and ran to Pinocchio.
Together, they lifted him above the happy crowd.
Pinocchio had never received such a warm welcome.
The puppets called him brother, cousin, and friend.
They danced while the musicians played faster.
The children clapped, although the play had stopped.
For a few minutes, Pinocchio forgot all his problems.
[with controlled thunder]
Then a heavy boot hit the floor backstage.
The music stopped, and every puppet became still.
The theatre belonged to a huge man called Fire Eater.
His black beard reached all the way down.
His eyes were red, and his voice was very loud.
“Who stopped my show?” he asked angrily.
The puppets pointed at Pinocchio with shaking hands.
Fire Eater picked him up and carried him backstage.
Pinocchio stopped feeling brave at once.
A large fire was cooking Fire Eater’s dinner.
But the weak flames could not finish the food.
Fire Eater looked at Pinocchio like dry firewood.
“A wooden puppet will burn well,” he said.
Pinocchio shook until his wooden joints made noise.
He called for Geppetto and spoke about the lost coat.
