[British accent]
[with rising suspense]
Two dark shapes moved behind him.
They wore bags over their heads.
“Give us your money,” one shape ordered.
Pinocchio knew the Fox’s walk and Cat’s voice.
He quickly put the four coins into his mouth.
The thieves caught him and pulled at his jaw.
Pinocchio closed his teeth and kicked hard.
One thief held his arms tightly.
The other thief reached near his mouth.
Pinocchio bit the paw, and the Cat cried out.
He pulled free and ran into the forest.
The two thieves followed close behind.
Pinocchio crossed water and jumped over roots.
The coins hit together behind his teeth.
[hushed and urgent]
Then he saw a small white house.
A child with blue hair stood at a window.
Pinocchio asked her to open the door quickly.
She said nobody alive was inside the house.
The window closed, and Pinocchio was alone.
He knocked again, but nobody opened the door.
The two thieves came into the garden.
They asked for the coins one more time.
Pinocchio could not speak with a full mouth.
They searched his clothes and wooden body.
But the thieves did not find the gold.
They decided to frighten him.
[solemnly]
They tied Pinocchio to a long rope.
Then they lifted him into a large oak tree.
Strong wind and rain moved the branches.
The thieves waited for Pinocchio to give up.
But he kept the coins inside his mouth.
Soon the thieves became cold and tired.
They promised to return with tools at sunrise.
Then they left Pinocchio alone in the storm.
[quietly, then hopefully]
The rope held him while the wind turned him.
He stopped thinking about the golden tree.
He thought about Geppetto waiting at home.
He remembered the coat and the lost book.
Every easy choice had taken him farther away.
“Father,” he tried to call through the coins.
Lightning showed the white house again.
A small blue light appeared behind one window.
Someone inside had finally seen him.
Pinocchio became weak as the storm continued.
His hands opened, and his head fell forwards.
The coins now felt empty and useless.
Then he heard large wings above the tree.
A great bird flew around the oak.
It called loudly towards the white house.
A door opened as the blue light grew.
At last, help was coming towards Pinocchio.
But could it reach him before sunrise?
