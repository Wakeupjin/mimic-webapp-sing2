[with dark humour]
Pinocchio returned to the sea before the officers came.
A strong current carried him behind some high rocks.
Then a large fishing net rose under him.
Pinocchio fell among fish, crabs, and eels.
A strange Green Fisherman owned the net.
He looked at Pinocchio like a rare fish.
“You will taste good fried,” said the Fisherman.
He carried the full net into a dark cave.
He covered each fish with white flour.
Pinocchio explained that he was a wooden puppet.
The Fisherman laughed and covered him with flour.
White dust reached from Pinocchio’s head to feet.
Hot oil moved loudly inside a large pan.
[with relief]
Suddenly, angry barking filled the cave.
Alidoro had followed Pinocchio’s smell to the rocks.
The dog jumped between Pinocchio and the pan.
The Fisherman turned, and Alidoro grabbed Pinocchio.
Alidoro carried him outside and ran away.
Pinocchio cleaned his face and thanked the dog.
“We saved each other today,” Alidoro replied.
He said Eugene was getting better now.
The other boys confessed, so Pinocchio was free.
Pinocchio felt relief, but the fight still shamed him.
He had not caused the injury, but he had still fought.
Alidoro left, and Pinocchio walked home alone.
It was night when he reached the Fairy’s door.
[wryly]
He knocked softly, loudly, and then very hard.
A Snail answered from a high window.
She said she would come down slowly.
Pinocchio waited outside for many cold hours.
At last, he kicked the door in anger.
His wooden foot broke through and became stuck.
He could not enter or walk away.
The Snail reached him when the sun rose.
She brought a breakfast that looked very good.
The bread was plaster, and the fruit was stone.
Pinocchio understood the painful joke while feeling hungry.
He fainted and later awoke inside the house.
The Fairy listened to his complete story.
[warmly]
Pinocchio admitted missing school, fighting, and kicking.
He did not blame only the other boys.
The Fairy repaired his foot, but not his responsibility.
For many months, Pinocchio went to school daily.
He studied, helped the Fairy, and chose kind friends.
When people teased him, he controlled his anger.
His teacher saw his slow and steady change.
At year’s end, Pinocchio was the best pupil.
The Fairy promised he would become a real boy.
She planned a party for all his friends.
Pinocchio imagined Geppetto smiling at the wonderful news.
He wanted to invite every friend himself.
He searched town for the lazy boy Lamp-Wick.
His dream was close, but new trouble waited.
