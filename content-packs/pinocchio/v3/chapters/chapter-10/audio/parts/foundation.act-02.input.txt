[with strange wonder]
He tied a heavy stone around Pinocchio’s neck.
He tied another rope around one back leg.
Pinocchio pulled away, but his leg was weak.
The man talked happily about his new drum.
Then he pushed the frightened donkey from the cliff.
Pinocchio fell through the air into cold water.
The stone pulled him towards the sea floor.
The man waited above and held the rope.
Pinocchio sank into dark and silent water.
He thought about Geppetto and his lost home.
Suddenly, many little fish swam around him.
They began eating the grey donkey covering.
They did not bite the wooden body inside.
Soon the long ears and tail were gone.
Pinocchio’s wooden arms and legs appeared again.
He moved his fingers and kicked both feet.
The Fairy’s magic had protected him again.
The man pulled up the rope at last.
A living wooden puppet came out of the sea.
[with rising hope]
The man opened his mouth in great surprise.
“Where is my donkey?” he asked loudly.
“I am that donkey,” Pinocchio answered with a laugh.
He quickly told the man what had happened.
The angry man tried to catch him again.
Pinocchio removed the rope and jumped away.
His wooden body could swim very well.
He left the cliff far behind him.
A white rock stood ahead in the sunlight.
A small blue Goat waited on the rock.
She called to him in the Fairy’s voice.
“Swim faster, Pinocchio!” the Goat cried.
Then the water behind him made a terrible sound.
[with awe and dread]
Small fish rushed away from a huge shadow.
The Terrible Shark opened its cave-like mouth.
Water pulled Pinocchio towards its great teeth.
The blue Goat called again from the rock.
Pinocchio fought hard against the strong water.
The Shark swallowed him in one great rush.
Pinocchio fell into cold water and darkness.
[hushed, then determined]
He heard the Shark breathing all around him.
Pinocchio called out, and someone answered.
A large Tunny was floating close to him.
The Tunny had lost all hope of escape.
He told Pinocchio to accept the dark place.
Pinocchio would not give up so easily.
Then he saw a tiny golden light far away.
No sunlight could enter the Shark’s body.
“Someone may be there,” Pinocchio told the Tunny.
He thanked the fish and walked towards the light.
The path was wet, cold, and completely dark.
Still, the little light grew brighter with every step.
Pinocchio was no longer running towards easy fun.
This time, he was trying to find his way home.
