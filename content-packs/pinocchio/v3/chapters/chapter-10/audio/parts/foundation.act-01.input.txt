[with dawning concern]
Pinocchio woke the next morning as a grey donkey.
Lamp-Wick stood beside him with his ears down.
Both boys felt afraid and deeply ashamed.
The Little Man kicked open the bedroom door.
He smiled as if nothing bad had happened.
Then he brushed their coats until they looked bright.
The boys tried to speak, but only brayed.
The Little Man led them to a busy market.
Selling careless boys was his secret business.
A farmer bought Lamp-Wick for work on his farm.
A circus owner came to look at Pinocchio.
He checked the donkey’s legs, teeth, and eyes.
Then he bought Pinocchio and pulled him away.
[briskly]
The circus stable was cold and smelled of hay.
Pinocchio did not want to eat the dry food.
His new master became angry and punished him.
Every day, Pinocchio had to learn new tricks.
He bowed, danced, and counted to the music.
He walked on two legs around the ring.
He also jumped through hoops and over boards.
Bright posters soon covered walls across the town.
They promised a donkey who danced like a man.
One evening, many families filled the circus tent.
Pinocchio entered with ribbons and a small hat.
The crowd cheered, but he felt trapped inside.
The music started, and Pinocchio gave a deep bow.
[longingly]
He marched in circles and balanced a glass.
Then he looked towards a seat near the front.
A beautiful lady wore his picture around her neck.
Soft blue hair showed under her silver veil.
Pinocchio knew that she was the Blue Fairy.
Sudden hope made him forget his next trick.
“Fairy, I am here!” he tried to shout.
His mouth made only a loud donkey sound.
The crowd laughed because they thought it was funny.
The Fairy looked sad and left her seat.
Pinocchio kept watching the empty place.
The circus owner shouted for the final jump.
Pinocchio ran towards a bright paper hoop.
[with restrained alarm]
His front feet hit it, and he fell.
A sharp pain moved through his left leg.
The music stopped, and the people became quiet.
The owner only cared about the broken show.
An animal doctor checked Pinocchio the next morning.
“This donkey will always walk badly,” he said.
The owner decided to sell Pinocchio at once.
Most buyers did not want an injured donkey.
At last, a man who made drums stopped.
He looked closely at Pinocchio’s thick skin.
The man wanted the skin for a drum.
He bought Pinocchio for only four small coins.
The man took him to a cliff by the sea.
