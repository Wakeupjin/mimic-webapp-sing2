# Pinocchio v3 Story Bible

## Promise

A newly carved boy mistakes freedom for doing whatever he wants, then slowly
learns that love, courage, truth, and responsibility are what make him real.

The twelve Chapters form one complete story. Each Chapter must also have its own
desire, complication, consequence, and forward pull so it works as a class.

## Canon

- Pinocchio begins impulsive, curious, funny, and emotionally young. He is not
  cruel; he fails because desire reaches him before judgment does.
- Geppetto is poor, proud, inventive, and consistently loving. His sacrifices
  have material weight.
- The Talking Cricket tells inconvenient truths without becoming the narrator's
  moral megaphone.
- The Blue Fairy offers care and second chances, never consequence-free rescue.
- The Fox and the Cat manipulate Pinocchio through urgency, vanity, and easy
  rewards.
- Pinocchio's nose is a memorable consequence of lying, not the entire story.
- The ending is earned through repeated choices, work, courage, and care for
  Geppetto—not through a single magical reward.

## Voice and tone

Warm British storybook narration with human timing, light wit, vivid action,
and emotional restraint. The narration may smile, worry, or wonder, but must not
sound like a trailer announcer or a nursery robot. Dialogue should be playable
without turning every character into a caricature.

Core language uses natural contemporary English while preserving the Tuscan
setting and nineteenth-century story world. Avoid slang that dates the pack,
abstract moral lectures, padded description, and repeated plot summaries.

## Learning continuity

- Watch carries the whole Chapter narrative.
- Mimic selects thirty exact, high-value sentences across all beats.
- Guess tests visual prediction, event retrieval, and simple inference.
- Word rebuilds ten exact sentences that reward grammar and chunk awareness.
- Foundation and Studio preserve the same eight beats and consequences as Core.
- A level change must never change who acted, what happened, or why the next
  Chapter begins.

## Twelve-Chapter arc

1. A Voice in the Wood — the wood speaks, Geppetto creates Pinocchio, and the
   new boy escapes while Geppetto pays the price.
2. Hunger, Home, and Sacrifice — hunger and the Cricket expose consequences;
   Geppetto returns, feeds Pinocchio, and sacrifices his coat for school.
3. The Puppet Theater — Pinocchio trades school for spectacle, then protects a
   friend and receives five gold coins.
4. Five Coins and a Dangerous Road — the Fox and Cat sell an easy fortune;
   danger catches Pinocchio before he can return home.
5. The Blue Fairy and the Field of Wonders — care, lies, the growing nose, and
   another bad choice make trust the central conflict.
6. The Snake, the Trap, and the Dog — punishment, work, and an unexpected act
   of loyalty show the first signs of change.
7. The Sea and the Terrible Dogfish — Pinocchio searches for Geppetto and sees
   how far his choices have carried both of them.
8. The Land of Busy Bees — honest work and reunion with the Fairy make a better
   life possible, but discipline still has to become a habit.
9. The Land of Toys — a final promise is traded for endless play, and pleasure
   without responsibility reveals its cost.
10. Donkey Days — Pinocchio is exploited, injured, and thrown into the sea,
    where he regains his wooden form but loses his freedom again.
11. Inside the Dogfish — Pinocchio finds Geppetto and must become the brave,
    practical child his father needs.
12. A Real Boy — escape, work, care, and restitution complete the transformation
    that magic alone could never provide.

## Editorial invariants

- Original Chapters 1–36 are covered once across the season map.
- Each Core master aims for 6–10 minutes, with 8 minutes preferred where the
  source has enough story capacity.
- Short source material belongs in a serial or anthology season; never inflate a
  thin story to ninety minutes.
- New facts, characters, or reordered outcomes require an explicit Story Bible
  revision before batch production.
