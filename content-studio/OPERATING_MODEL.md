# Mimic AI-Native Operating Model

## Product definition

Mimic is not a web-app project. It is a monthly content operation:

> Every month, learners play with one film world and one story world in English.

The learner promise is a twelve-class Monthly Pack. A long story may fill the
whole season; medium or short tales are bundled without padding them. Both film
and story use the same Watch → Mimic → Guess → Word learning engine.

## Four operating systems

### Content OS

Every film and story is a versioned pack with rights, source, full narrative,
selected curriculum, media provenance, QA, cost, and performance data. The
repository is canonical until the schema is stable enough to justify a CMS.

### Product OS

Content is data, not hard-coded UI. Preview, staging, and production are
separate. Automated validation and review precede release, and every published
pack can roll back to an immutable previous version.

### Revenue OS

The initial route is academy- and tutor-led B2B2C: an institution base fee plus
active-student pricing. Mimic's own classes remain the product laboratory.
Licensed film minimum guarantees are accepted only when committed revenue can
reasonably recover them.

### Risk OS

The company owns code, domain, brand, content, vendor accounts, and contracts.
It maintains a rights registry, privacy and deletion rules, access control,
backups, incident procedures, and vendor provenance for every generated asset.

## Incorporation trigger

Form the operating company before the first film-license master agreement or
paid institutional contract. Transfer the founder-created code, brand, domain,
content, and vendor accounts through documented corporate ownership rather
than leaving them under personal control.

## Ninety-day sequence

### Days 0–30 — leave personal-project mode

- Establish the Golden Chapter and Story Pack standard.
- Inventory rights, accounts, secrets, contracts, and founder-created IP.
- Separate preview, staging, and production; stop direct unreviewed releases.
- Add backup/restore, rollback, error monitoring, and operating runbooks.
- Build a Monthly Pack contribution-margin model.

Exit test: another operator can deploy and roll back within fifteen minutes.

### Days 31–90 — build the content factory

- Run rights → content → product QA as three separate gates.
- Produce two months of inventory ahead of release.
- Add Admin v1 for pack state, access, progress, billing exceptions, and audit.
- Secure three paying pilot institutions and measure real classroom completion.

Exit test: a new content manager can take the next Story Pack to staging
without live founder instruction.

### Months 3–6 — create the data flywheel

Record pack, version, level, chapter, mode, and sentence on learning events.
Use completion, replay, error, and churn signals to revise the next immutable
pack version. Run a quarterly “founder absent for two weeks” operating drill.

## First hires

1. Content Operations Lead — an AI-orchestrating editor, not a volume copywriter
2. Product/AI Engineer — release, data, security, and production automation
3. Customer Success — after repeatable paid institutional adoption
4. Rights/BizOps — after licensing volume justifies an internal owner

Legal, accounting, and specialist design remain external early on.

## Company metrics

- gross profit per employee;
- paying active students and institution renewal;
- Monthly Pack on-time release and twelve-class completion;
- human minutes and cash cost per finished audio minute;
- contribution margin after film licensing;
- Story Pack reuse across levels, courses, and markets;
- release-ready content inventory in months;
- production content-defect escape rate.

## Company-ready test

Mimic is operating as a company when a non-founder can produce and publish the
next pack, roll back a faulty release, rotate a vendor key, resolve a progress
issue, process a billing exception, and identify the rights evidence without
depending on private founder knowledge.
